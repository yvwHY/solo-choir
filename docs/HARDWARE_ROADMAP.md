# Solo Choir — Hardware Roadmap (Track B: the wearable)

*The embodied half of the thesis, parallel to the software instrument (Track A). Software produces the choir; hardware delivers it into / from the singer's body. Draft — refine as experiments and supervisor input land.*

> **Framing updated 2026-06-23 to match `SOLO_CHOIR_RESEARCH_VISION.md`.** Bone conduction returns to the **core** as the *learn-mode* (delivering an independent harmony target into the head, open ear). The DAEX mouth-rig is reframed as the *perform-mode* **embodied / spatial source** — **not** a formant filter (that claim is retracted; see §3). ---

## 1. Goal

Deliver the AI-generated choir **into and from the singer's body** during practice and performance, so harmony is *learned in the body* and *performed from the body* — "the body as an interface, a choir of one." Two modes of one idea:

- **Learn in-body** — bone conduction (open ear) puts an *independent* harmony target into the head while the singer still hears their own real voice → train the interval by feel.
- **Perform from-body** — the choir radiates *from the singer's own mouth/body*, co-located and blended with the live voice, so the choir reads as coming from one person.

## 3. Key findings (why the framing is what it is)

- **Bone conduction must go on BONE, not the neck.** The **Dayton DAEX25CT-4 cannot penetrate neck soft tissue** — structure-borne vibration into soft tissue is heavily lossy (the same physics that makes pressing an exciter to the cheek fail to drive the oral cavity). So bone-conduction delivery goes on the **cheekbone / mastoid** (where commercial bone-conduction headphones sit), not the neck.
- **The throat-mic + bone-conduction combo has precedent and is feasible.** Military / high-noise comms pair bone-conduction output with throat / contact mics and work in 100+ dB noise (INVISIO, theBoom, EarHugger). It works not because tissue "kills" the vibration (cross-skull bone conduction has only ~0–10 dB interaural attenuation; soft tissue damps only ~5 dB), but because the contact mic sits on the larynx at high level and the output reaching it is attenuated by the TMJ + soft tissue + distance.
- **The real residual risk was *Beatrice self-stacking*, not a squeal.** Because a neural converter is in the loop, even low-level leakage gets re-converted and compounds gradually. **Test A (feedback / leakage threshold) was run 2026-06-27 and passed by ear** (§7): no squeal at realistic placements and no self-stacking on sustained singing. Risk retired for the viva (instrumented gain-sweep optional for a quotable number).
- **DAEX-mouth is embodiment, not timbre.** Genuine mouth-formant-filtering needs a talkbox tube *into* the mouth; just radiating at / pressing on the face gives negligible filtering, and **software (Beatrice) already owns timbre controllably**. So timbre stays a software job; the mouth-rig's claim is *spatial / embodied source*.
- **The DAEX needs a coupling surface/cavity to radiate — confirmed by ear (2026-06-24, first bench test for §7 Test B).** Free-air (exciter bonded to nothing) it is muffled and the harmony separation collapses (high partials don't radiate → parts smear together). Bonded to a small rigid cavity (a cylindrical cup) it opens up to roughly Mac-speaker clarity and the harmony reads again. The harmony is fully present in the signal — throat-mic → **Mac speaker** is clear *with* harmony — so this is purely an acoustic-coupling effect, not a signal/engine issue. This **validates the perform-mode "DAEX on a rigid cup" design**: the cup/cavity *is* the radiator, and its material / size / coupling tightness is the timbre lever (stiffer+lighter → better highs; cavity size tunes which bands resonate). Bass stays weak (small cavity), so upper parts (Alto/Sop) reproduce better than Bass on the DAEX. Signal chain used: throat mic → **Mac built-in TRRS jack** (the throat mic is 4-pole TRRS — it failed in the USB card's 3-pole TRS mic jack, near-silent; the Mac combo jack accepts it) → engine → USB card line-out → PAM8403 → DAEX.

## 4. Components in play

- **Bone-conduction exciter:** Dayton **BCE-1** (learn-mode, on cheek/mastoid) — or a commercial bone-conduction headset (simpler, but its own latency/control tradeoffs; confirm transducer choice).
- **Surface exciter:** **DAEX25CT-4** (perform-mode mouth-rig, on a rigid cup).
- **Amplifier:** **PAM8403** (stereo 2×3W class-D) — one board can drive **two** transducers: `OUT_L → DAEX`, `OUT_R → bone-conduction`. Two bone-conduction BCE-1s on one channel must be **series** (not parallel) — see the 2026-07-01 build-log entry.
- **Microcontroller:** Raspberry Pi **Pico W** (on-body I/O / wireless control).
- **Input:** **Topiky covert throat mic** (CTIA 4-pole 3.5mm headset: throat mic + acoustic-tube earpiece + PTT) — primary; piezo as fallback. (An **intraoral mic** is a separate, sign-off-gated path.)
- **USB sound card:** **UGREEN USB→3.5mm dual-jack** (separate pink mic-in + green headphone-out; macOS plug-and-play, supplies mic bias). Reported as `USB Audio Device` (IN×1 / OUT×2).
- **Sealing / wear:** Ecoflex 00-50 (face-contact seal), head/neck straps. Haptic vibration motor(s) for the fallback path (driver TBD).

## 5. How the software feeds the hardware

Track A's engine output is the source signal for the wearable:

```
voice in ──► [software instrument: Beatrice convert + diatonic/MIDI harmony] ──► audio out
                                                                              │
                                                              amp (PAM8403)   │  L → DAEX (perform)
                                                                              ▼  R → bone conduction (learn)
                                                   transducer(s) ──► into / from the body
```

- **Learn-mode** routes an *independent* target (a recorded SATB stem, or a MIDI/score line) to bone conduction — see Track A. The recorded stem is a frozen target, so no MIDI is strictly required for the learning use.
- **Perform-mode:** **"You" is OFF on-body** (the real acoustic voice is the unison), so the engine only adds the *harmony* voices → fewer inference passes → more real-time headroom. Live ≈ ≤ 3 parts on CPU; full simultaneous SATB = offline render.
- Latency through the hardware chain (amp + transducer) adds to software latency — measure **end-to-end**, not just software.

## 8. Firmware & division of labour

- Any **Pico W firmware** (on-body audio routing / wireless control) may live in this repo later — if so, in scope for Claude Code under the same guardrails (small steps, no DSP changes, no restricted binaries committed).
- **Physical making** (soldering, fitting transducers, wearable form, the mouth-rig cup, sealing) is human + workshop + supervisor — not Claude Code's task. Use this doc to plan it; keep build notes and measurements here as they accumulate.
- **Remaining pre-viva hardware item (2026-06-27):** with Test A passed and the electronics/signal chain proven, the one outstanding piece is an **enclosure / mount** that holds the components (DAEX cup, bone-conduction transducer, throat-mic, PAM8403, wiring) in fixed positions on the body. This also enforces the decoupling discipline (output and throat-mic not sharing rigid structure) by design rather than by hand-holding.
- **Enclosure material — changed from 3D print to wire + fibre (2026-06-28).** The mask form is now a **wire armature wrapped in paper fibre / other hard fibre**, hardened with glue/resin (PVA / shellac / resin), instead of 3D-printed plastic. Conceptually stronger than printing — handmade, organic, body-extension (Rebecca Horn), and directly continuous with the user's textile/fibre background → a distinctive maker line for the CV/SoP. Three engineering constraints the material switch must NOT break (all follow from §3 / §7):
  1. **The DAEX coupling cup must stay rigid + light.** §3 (2026-06-24) validated that the exciter only radiates clear, separated harmony off a *rigid* cup, and stiffer+lighter → better highs. Resin-hardened wound fibre can be both stiff and light (possibly better than printed plastic here), **but the cup specifically must end up genuinely hard** — don't leave it compliant.
  2. **Wire is an excellent structure-borne conductor → decoupling matters MORE, not less.** If the wire armature rigidly links the DAEX cup to the throat-mic / bone-conduction transducer, vibration travels the frame → feedback / Beatrice self-stacking (exactly what Test A guards against, §7). Keep the "**one shell, two mechanically-isolated islands**" rule: break the frame between the two zones with Ecoflex / sorbothane / an air gap — **the wire must not run continuously across both islands.**
  3. **Weight / hygiene:** wound fibre is usually lighter than printed plastic (more comfortable to sing in); face-contact surfaces still need the Ecoflex 00-50 seal (§4) + disposable contact pads for public exhibition (see exhibition hygiene plan).
- **Material decision — millinery wire (form) + elastic band (bone-conduction pressure) (2026-06-28).** Two roles, opposite needs, so split the materials:
  - **Structural form** = **millinery wire (cotton-covered steel) + fibre wrapping** — light, hand-bendable, *holds shape* (low spring-back is what you want for the form); the cotton wrap grips fibre + glue and matches the textile aesthetic. (Aluminium armature wire is an alternative.) This wire holds the mask shape and carries the electronics.
  - **Bone-conduction contact force** = an **adjustable elastic band** that presses the bone-conduction transducer onto the bone in front of the ear, with an Ecoflex/silicone pad at the skin (§4). Bone conduction needs *steady static contact pressure* (too loose → weak coupling / smeared harmony; too tight → painful); the elastic supplies an adjustable, comfortable pressure.
  - **Why elastic over a spring-steel arm:** the elastic is **non-structure-borne**, so the contact force comes from fabric/rubber, NOT from the metal armature — this *automatically* satisfies constraint 2 above (the wire frame must not carry transducer vibration back to the throat-mic → feedback / self-stacking). A spring-steel arm would give better return but would still need separate isolation; the elastic *is* the isolation. The transducer should hang mainly from the elastic, not be clamped rigidly to the wire — leave a soft pad (Ecoflex/sorbothane) at any wire junction.
  - **Caveat:** elastic relaxes with time/sweat, so contact pressure drops on long wear. Fine for exhibition (short, rotating wear); for long practice sessions, add an adjustable buckle or a spring-steel arm later.
- **Fibre wrap + hardener — two zones, different needs (2026-06-28).** The skin over the wire armature:
  - **Main shell (form-led: light, organic, textile-aligned):** layered **mulberry/washi paper** (best strength-to-weight, like dry lacquer) *or* **jute/cotton cord wrapping** (matches the textile background) *or* papier-mâché (cheapest). Harden with **shellac** (natural, more sweat/damp-resistant than PVA) or **PVA** (lightest, but softens with sweat/humidity — OK for short rotating exhibition wear) — or **model dope (nitrocellulose)** for a wire-frame + paper-skin shell that pulls taut and stiffens very light + rigid.
  - **DAEX cup (no compromise — must be genuinely rigid + light; §3 validated stiffer+lighter → better highs):** PVA-only paper is **too soft** here. Use **layered fibre (washi / cheesecloth) + epoxy resin**, or bond in a ready-made hard cup. The cup is the radiator — if it is soft the harmony separation collapses, so accept a little extra weight for stiffness.
  - **Face-contact** stays Ecoflex/silicone (§4); **safety:** epoxy and dope need ventilation + gloves/mask when curing.
- **Final purchased material list + build process (2026-06-29).** Sourced on Amazon UK:
  - Armature: **OLYCRAFT 1mm millinery wire** (PVC-covered iron — fine, since fibre is wrapped over it so adhesion is fibre-to-fibre, not glue-to-wire).
  - Shell fibre: **2mm paper twine** + **A4 mulberry/washi paper** (paper bonds well with water-based glue; a waxed cord was rejected — wax repels water-based adhesive).
  - Shell hardener: **PVA (on hand)** for layered build + **water-based shellac sanding sealer** as the moisture-seal top coat (water-based = non-toxic / no fumes, safer than spirit shellac for a shared space).
  - DAEX cup hardener: **epoxy resin (on hand)** — kept separate from the shell so the cup is genuinely rigid (§3).
  - Bone-conduction pressure: **elastic band (on hand)**.
  - **Build order:** bend wire armature → wrap paper twine / mulberry + **diluted PVA (~1:1 water)**, build 3–5 layers, letting each dry → **water-based shellac seal** (1–2 coats) over fully-dry PVA (don't seal damp PVA — traps moisture, softens). DAEX cup done separately in epoxy. Face contact = Ecoflex.
  - **Still to confirm:** Ecoflex 00-50 (§4) for the skin-contact seal — final assembly layer, not needed to start the shell.

### 8a. Mesh-textile wearable — material options (2026-08-03)

A second wearable line opened 2026-08-01, separate from the mask: **a 3D-printed mesh "fabric" with a rigid plate for the exciter**, the plate's surroundings graduating into open mesh. Coupons live in `prints/260801_mesh_coupon/` (v2 slit-based → v3/v5 rod-based re-entrant honeycomb → v4 staggered kirigami; see that README for geometry, print numbers and test results).

**Where it stands:** PLA coupons are all **too stiff** — v2 could not be opened at all, v3 only barely. Softening is proceeding through geometry (v5: bigger cells + thinner sheet), with no-plate control coupons cut to isolate whether the rigid plate is the culprit.

**Material options, in the order they should be tried:**

| Material | Forming | Why | Watch out |
|---|---|---|---|
| **PLA (on hand)** | Thermoforms at Tg ≈ 60–65 °C — hot water, then press onto a curved form | Zero cost, 40 min/iteration, already in hand. **Heat-setting sidesteps the "mechanism too stiff to pull open" problem entirely** — the sheet doesn't need to deploy, it can be shaped | Anneals/embrittles if overheated; heat gun scorches thin rods — use a water bath |
| **Leather (vegetable-tanned)** | **Wet forming** — case in water, shape on a form, dry 24–48 h, permanent | See below | Laser cut only ⇒ uniform thickness; shrinks on drying; not re-printable if the geometry is wrong |
| **Thermoplastic medical mesh** (Varaform / X-Lite / Aquaplast) | ~70 °C water | Already a mesh, already designed to form on a human body; common in theatre/SFX so the workshop may hold it | Mesh pattern is fixed — no custom auxetic geometry; would serve as a substrate, with the printed plate bonded on |
| **Fosshape 300/600** | Steam / ~90 °C | Thermoplastic non-woven, **sewable** | Stiffens by shrinking — dimensions shift |
| **SLS nylon PA11/PA12** (outsourced) | — | Isotropic (no FDM layer-splitting in thin rods), 15–25 % elongation at break, no stringing/support problems; the real process behind the reference imagery | £20–40/sheet, 1–2 week lead time ⇒ **for the final part, not for iteration** |
| **TPU on the MINI IS** | — | Solves stiffness and fracture outright | Bowden extruder: needs 95A, 15–20 mm/s, and retraction barely works ⇒ **stringing webs the lattice holes shut**. Also creeps under sustained load (bad for a worn part) |

**On leather specifically** — worth taking seriously for four reasons:
1. **Wet forming is the mature answer to "mouldable"** — centuries-old technique, permanent set, and unlike PLA it does not embrittle.
2. **Laser cutting suits auxetic geometry well.** The in-plane rod-width taper of v3/v5 transfers directly; only z-direction variation is lost. Thickness sold by ounce (1 oz ≈ 0.4 mm), so **2 oz ≈ 0.8 mm matches the current geometry**.
3. **Toughness far above PLA** — no brittle fracture, good flex-cycle life, which is exactly where the PLA coupons are expected to fail.
4. **It continues the same narrative line as the mask's wire + fibre decision (§8)** — handmade, organic, body-extension, continuous with the textile/pattern-cutting background. Arguably a better fit than printed plastic.

**Unknown that must be measured:** leather's damping is far higher than PLA's. For a mesh surrounding an exciter plate that could either help (energy stays in the plate rather than leaking into the mesh) or hurt (the whole thing goes dull). The coupon acceptance list already carries this test — run it on leather against PLA and bare plate.

**Post-processing — joining and edge finishing (2026-08-09).** Coupons print as separate tiles (150×150, bed-limited) with deliberately open left/right edges (README §v3: half-cut diagonal rods are expected), so both a seam method and an edge treatment are needed for any part bigger than a coupon.

The governing rule for both: **the seam/edge must be at least as compliant as the mesh.** The sheet is already too stiff (above); a continuous rigid line at the boundary concentrates all strain into the adjacent row of 0.8 mm rods, which is then the first thing to break. This rules out the obvious answers — CA glue (brittle, and capillary action wicks along the rods and fuses neighbouring cells shut, the same failure v2 logged as "pre-opening welds it solid") and solvent welding (PLA needs DCM/THF, not acetone; same wicking, plus toxicity). Epoxy is on hand but belongs to the DAEX cup (§8), never the mesh.

- **Joining, in order:** ① lace/sew the butted edges (waxed thread or 0.3 mm mono; zero added stiffness, reversible, and a faggoting/insertion stitch is the textile-native form of exactly this joint) → ② design the join into the geometry — terminate the edge row in open loops so tiles interlink chainmail-style, no adhesive at all → ③ **soldering-iron spot welds at nodes only** — same-material, strongest per point, but the bead is locally stiff so it must sit where rods already meet, never mid-rod where bending happens → ④ flexible adhesive lap joint (E6000/PU class) or a VHB strip as a viscoelastic splice; last resort, adds mass and closes cells.
- **DONE 2026-08-09: ③ used — tiles heat-welded with a soldering iron.** Seam flex-cycle durability not yet tested; that is the open question for this method.
- **Edge finishing — sanding does not work on the mesh.** No backing behind a 0.6–0.8 mm rod (it deflects away, or snaps), and PLA's low Tg means friction heat smears it and clogs the abrasive. **The right tool for thin PLA is heat, not abrasion:** flush-cut each burr, then touch the stub with the iron tip (~1 s) so it beads over. Alternatives: hot-water hem-folding at 60–65 °C (textile-native, but the fold is locally stiff and repeated heating anneals/embrittles — same caveat as the PLA row above), or bind the edge with a leather/tape strip (also solves skin comfort — cut rod ends scratch). **Best answer is upstream: print a selvedge** so no rod is cut mid-span — but segmented or serpentine, never a continuous hoop, which would box in the expansion the open edges were left open to reveal.
- **Sanding is fine on the ⌀55 plate** (1.6 mm solid): wet-sand 240→400→800. Do not polish the exciter-bonding face (240 keys it for adhesion) and do not thin the plate — thickness is an acoustic parameter.
- **Tool hygiene:** an iron tip used for plastic welding usually loses its plating for soldering. Clean while hot at ~200 °C by wiping on dry paper (never sandpaper or steel wool — that strips the plating permanently), then re-tin with rosin-core solder. Dedicate a spare tip to plastic; the unit-1 rework (§4a) needs a tip that still wets.

- **The LiPo-powered units trip their protection under channel routing; diagnosed live on 2026-08-18.** Symptom: unit 1 and unit 2, each with its own LiPo through TP4056, MT3608 and PAM8403, cut out mid-phrase under channel routing, and **unplugging and reconnecting the LiPo revives them at once**, which means the protection board (a DW01-class part) has **latched** on over-current or under-voltage rather than the battery being flat; a flat battery does not revive on reconnection. Both tripped independently, which makes it systematic: with the channels routed, one unit carries the sustained power of a whole part on its own, and the bass at +7 dB and the tenor at +6 dB of trim are the hardest worked, so the current peaks pull the voltage below the protection threshold. **Decision: the rig form moves to mains power** — the existing rule of 07-14 is that rig amplifiers run from a mains USB charger of 3 A or more, with LiPo reserved for the worn mode — which sidesteps the problem entirely. A capacitor, a bulk electrolytic across the PAM VCC, is not added for now: it holds up only an instantaneous peak and not a sustained passage, and on mains the problem does not exist. It is left for wearable testing after the viva, and if one or two units trip on the body then, a capacitor plus two LiPos in parallel can be soldered in one go. An untried software option: a per-channel attenuation flag in the engine to lower the level on the LiPo channels, at the cost of that part becoming quieter and the balance needing to be judged again.

**Related context:** Jenny Huang's RCA MA Fashion 2023 project *Auxetic Patterning* builds garments from entirely flat sheets via auxetic geometry, deployed into pre-programmed 3D structures by the wearer's body. The underlying maths is Konaković-Luković et al., *Rapid Deployment of Curved Surfaces via Programmable Auxetics* (SIGGRAPH 2018) — in-plane rotation of triangles gives isotropic area change, and **spatially varying the cell scale in the flat state programs which doubly-curved surface it deploys into**. This reframes the slit-based family (v2): its value is flat→doubly-curved deployment, not in-plane stretch — which is not how v2 was tested. **Do not attempt the full inverse solve before the viva**; a hand-designed expansion gradient is enough.
